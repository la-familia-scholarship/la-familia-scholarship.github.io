La Familia Community Scholarship

Strategy & Game Plan

The plan we are running.

The choices and the structure behind the work — always reflecting the current state.

Vision and mission.

Vision. A community investing in its own young adults, summer by summer — family, not philanthropy. The scholarship is one important initiative, not the point. The point is a circle of friends, peers, and grown men with that same path behind them, showing up for the young adults around them.

Mission. La Familia formalizes the support layer around more than a decade of organic mentorship that grew on a court in our community. We provide scholarship support and shared experiences to a known group of young adults, and we close the loop with the people who fund the work.

For the fuller story behind the work, see The Initiative.

The committee.

Mario Ramos — Chairperson. Champions the initiative. Holds the relationships with the young adults and their families. Leads disbursement decisions with the committee. Mario's time is evenings and weekends around a full-time job — the committee carries the operational lift so Mario's hands stay on relationships and judgment, where they belong.

The members.

Munchie · Rob · Ray · Rey · Justin · Elliott · Jesus · Tony

The committee spans walks of life — first responders (active and retired police and firefighters), tradesmen, and tech, real-estate, and business professionals. That range is the credibility asset. These are not "mentors." These are men from across the community, showing up.

Pedro Rosa — Facilitator. Operational, technology, and project-management support. Maintains the canonical artifacts, the ledger, and the site.

How we decide. We decide together. Material decisions move through equal committee-member support. Championed by Mario, owned by the members.

Operating year (founding). Friends-and-family, informal. The only formalization expected this year is to instantiate an LLC and open a bank account under the LLC. Beyond that is deferred.

Who we serve.

The founding cohort: seven young adults. Identified, known, and earned. These are not aspirational picks. They are the young adults who have shown up year after year — on the court and in the moments that matter — with consistency and earnestness. They span high school through college.

Selection philosophy. Participation and consistency, not a formal screen. The cohort came together by word of mouth. As new participants prove the same consistency, the circle grows; as the current seven move through school and college, new faces make their way in.

Geography. Across the Chicago metropolitan area, with a base on the Northwest side and reach to Hyde Park and beyond.

The application. A scholarship application functions as each young adult's own commitment — completed and signed before support is delivered.

Scholarship channels.

Support is disbursed through three channels, age-gated so funds reach a young adult in the way that makes the most sense.

Cash to the parent. For anyone under 18 or not yet graduated from high school — including minors going on trips or summer programs. Funds go to the parent, in the young adult's name.

Cash to the student. For students who are 18 or older and in college, managing their own affairs — for example, around $500 toward books, gear, or college expenses, used at the student's discretion.

Direct payment to a vendor. For specific initiative costs — college-visit travel, books, program fees. The scholarship pays the vendor directly.

Distribution philosophy. Per-student amounts are at the discretion of Mario and the committee — need-, age-, and goal-aware. What a graduating senior weighing college visits needs is different from what a high schooler joining the summer trip needs. The committee allocates by judgment, not even split.

Programmatic initiatives.

Beyond the scholarship itself, La Familia executes three programmatic initiatives in the founding summer. The intent is presence — being in the moments that shape a young adult's trajectory.

A game together (June – early July). The cohort to a live game in the Chicago area — a shared memory outside the court and outside the home.

A summer trip (anytime through the summer). A trip outside Chicago, chaperoned by the committee. For many of the seven, the first trip of its kind.

College visits (June – early July). Campus visits for students considering college, and for underclassmen and summer-program participants. Walking a campus changes what feels possible.

Budget. Overall and per-initiative budgets are an active action item.

Funding model.

One-time annual contributions. Contributions are one-time and annual. No recurring mechanism, no contracts, no formal pledges this founding year.

The ask: $50. Small enough to be a low barrier, meaningful at scale. From friends and family who believe in this work.

Individual ceiling: $1,000. We accept up to $1,000 from any single donor this founding year. The ceiling is not a limit on belief — it is a deliberate scaling constraint. We are in a learning year and we are keeping the operation intimate.

Baseline target: ~$2,500. A target to work toward, not a cap. A current working figure, to be revisited as initiative budgets are finalized.

Larger commitments. A markedly larger commitment — in the $4,000–$5,000 range or above — would trigger added rigor around legal and tax structure before it is accepted. Record-keeping today is designed to support that transition without rework.

Better to do right by these young adults than to grow before we are ready.

Donor experience.

The donor experience is about feeling part of something good — human and relational, not bureaucratic. We close the loop with every donor.

Records. A ledger of who gave and how much, and a separate record of what the young adults did with the support.

Receipt. Each donor receives a receipt stating the amount, expressing gratitude, and noting that the contribution is made with the understanding that there is no tax deduction or exemption at this time. This is an acknowledgment, not a tax document.

Touchpoints. A personal acknowledgment when a gift comes in, a midsummer update, and a post-summer recap.

Optional, never pressured. Where it fits, a supported young adult may send a brief, positive note back — framed as receptivity, never as an obligation.

Operating structure.

Structure. An LLC with a bank account under the LLC (Chase).

Phase 1 (near term). Donors are directed to a committee member, who shares the details to send a Zelle payment to the LLC's Chase account; the committee follows up with a receipt. Donors may also Zelle the account directly.

Phase 2 (planned). A Stripe-connected donation button on a public donation site.

All contributions carry the friends-and-family disclaimer noted above.

Where this is going.

Year-round, not summer-only. The committee is committed to this work year-round, not to a single summer. The founding year is a starting point, not a season.

Momentum is already real. Giving has begun organically — committee members talking to their networks, people contributing on the spot. The structure is catching up to the energy.

Deeper formalization, when it makes sense. Beyond the founding-year LLC and bank account, evaluating legal and tax structure is deferred to a future session — or sooner, if a larger commitment makes it the right call.

Cohort over crowd, for now. We are not optimizing for scale. We are optimizing to do this right by the young adults in front of us, and to learn what we need to learn before we grow.

This is the plan. The rest is showing up.
