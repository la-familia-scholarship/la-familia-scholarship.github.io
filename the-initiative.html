La Familia Community Scholarship
Basketball is the front door. Powerful mentorship is the next room.

A community investing in its own young adults — summer by summer.

Family, not philanthropy.

This grew on a court. For more than a decade, a group of friends, peers, and grown men who have been where these young adults are now has been showing up — for the pickup games, and for the young adults who came to play. Mentorship found its way into the gaps: between drills, on rides home, in conversations in the parking lot.

La Familia is not the founding of something new. It is the formalization of something this community has been doing — together, quietly, consistently — for more than a decade.

What we're really building.

Young adults who feel seen and supported at the milestones that shape a life.

Donors who experience the tangible joy of being part of young adults evolving and exploring with their support.

A small, trusted circle that learns together what this can become.

Presence is the work.

A summer trip. A college visit. A game together. These aren't symbolic gestures. They are the everyday moments — and the outlier ones that stick — that change what a young adult believes is possible. Showing up consistently is the mechanism.

The circle.

The committee spans walks of life — first responders, tradesmen, and tech, real-estate, and business professionals, active and retired. That range is exactly what gives the work credibility with the young adults: these are real men from across the community, showing up.

Anyone who has been part of this work, or wants to be, has a seat at the table.

An incubator, on purpose.

The founding year is deliberately small. We are holding scale down to protect the learning curve — walk before we run. That is a feature, not a limitation. Better to do right by the young adults in front of us than to grow before we are ready.

What carries forward.

La Familia is not a program with a finish line. It is how this community has chosen to be with its own — quietly, consistently, for as long as it takes. The work is not building something big. It is keeping the doors open, summer after summer, for the young adults who walk through them.

A community coming together as family — enjoying the evolution of this next generation.
